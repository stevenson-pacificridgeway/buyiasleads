How Much Do Annuity Leads Cost?

By Gregory Stevenson, producing FIA agent & author of Index Annuity Secrets · Updated October 2026
Short answer: shared annuity leads can be a few dollars each, while exclusive fixed indexed annuity (FIA) leads typically run from about $15 to $50+ each. The price gap is almost entirely about exclusivity and quality — and because a single annuity case pays a large commission, the more expensive exclusive lead usually returns more.

“How much do annuity leads cost?” is the first question most agents ask — and the honest answer is that price alone tells you very little. A $3 lead and a $40 lead are not the same product. Here's what actually drives the number, and what you should expect to pay in 2026.

Exclusive vs. shared: the price split

There are really two markets. Shared leads are sold to several agents at once, which is why they're cheap — but you're racing three to five other agents to the same phone. Exclusive leads go to one agent only, so they cost more per lead and convert far better. (We break this down in Exclusive vs. Shared Annuity Leads.)

Exclusive + nurturedHigher per leadIntroduced under your name and kept warm for you

What drives the price up or down

  • Exclusivity — one agent vs. many is the single biggest factor.
  • How the lead was generated — a real opt-in from someone who asked for indexed-annuity info is worth far more than scraped or aged data.
  • Nurture — a lead that's introduced under your name and followed up for months costs more than a raw name and number, and contacts at a higher rate.
  • Included tools — some programs bundle a CRM and delivery dashboard; others just hand you a spreadsheet.

The number that actually matters

Cost per lead is the wrong thing to optimize. What matters is return: because one FIA case can pay several thousand dollars in commission, even a modest close rate on exclusive leads can return several times the package price. A 100-lead package at $2,000 that produces three cases at ~$3,000 each returns roughly 4× — which is why the “expensive” lead is often the cheaper one in the end.
